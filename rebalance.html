Bring your portfolio back to plan

Markets don’t move your investments evenly. Over time, the asset that rallied grows bigger than you intended and quietly changes your risk. Rebalancing is the periodic act of selling a little of what grew too large and adding to what fell behind, so your mix matches the plan you chose.

Your planTarget mix
Equity 60%Debt 30%Gold 10%
After a bull runTwo years later
Equity 72%Debt 21%Gold 7%

Equity grew from 60% to 72% of the portfolio without you buying a single extra unit. A 30% market fall would now hurt noticeably more than the plan allowed for.

Why rebalance

Your asset allocation (how much sits in equity, debt, gold and so on) drives most of your portfolio’s risk and return. Rebalancing keeps that allocation honest.

Controls risk

Without rebalancing, a portfolio tends to drift toward its riskiest asset, because that’s the one that grows fastest in good years.

Buys low, sells high, by rule

You trim what has run up and add to what has lagged. It forces the discipline most investors struggle with emotionally.

Keeps goals on track

A goal five years away needs a different mix from one twenty years away. Rebalancing is also how you shift the mix as a goal nears.

Removes guesswork

A written rule (“rebalance when any asset is 5 points off”) replaces reacting to headlines.

When to rebalance

There are three common rules. All work; what matters is picking one and sticking to it.

Calendar

Rebalance on a fixed date, once or twice a year

For example, every April after the financial year starts, or on your birthday.

Simple and easy to remember, but may trade when drift is tiny, or wait too long after a sharp move.

Threshold

Rebalance when any asset moves beyond a band

For example, act when equity moves more than 5 percentage points away from its 60% target (below 55% or above 65%).

Responds to real drift, but needs regular monitoring and can trigger often in volatile years.
Practical default

Hybrid

Check on a fixed date, act only if outside the band

Review once a year (plus after big market moves), and rebalance only if an asset has drifted beyond 5 points.

Keeps checks rare and trades meaningful, which also keeps tax and exit-load costs low.

Also rebalance when your situation changes: a goal moving within 3–5 years, a new job, marriage, or retirement can each justify a new target mix.

How to rebalance, cheapest first

Every method below gets you back to target. They’re ordered by cost: try the first one before reaching for the next.

1

Redirect new money

Send fresh SIPs, bonuses or lumpsums only to the underweight asset until the mix is back on target. Pause or reduce SIPs into the overweight asset for a while.

No tax, no exit loadWorks best for small drifts or when you invest regularly. Large drifts can take many months to close.
2

Switch or STP within a fund house

Use a one-time Switch from the overweight scheme to the underweight scheme of the same AMC, or an STP to move it gradually over a few months.

Taxed as a saleEach switch-out is a redemption, so capital gains tax and exit load apply. No bank transfer is needed.
3

Redeem and reinvest

Redeem units of the overweight fund and make a fresh purchase in the underweight fund, which can be at a different AMC.

Taxed as a saleSame tax as a switch, plus a few days with money out of the market while the redemption is paid out.
4

Withdraw from the overweight side

If you’re drawing money (via SWP or a one-off redemption), take it from the asset that is above target.

Tax only on gains withdrawnIdeal for retirees: income and rebalancing happen in the same transaction.

Step-by-step process

Here’s the full routine, from listing holdings to placing the trades.

  1. Write down your target allocationFor example 60% equity, 30% debt, 10% gold, and a tolerance band such as ±5 points. Base it on goal horizon and risk tolerance.
  2. List every holding at today’s valueUse your CAS (Consolidated Account Statement) from CAMS, KFintech or the depositories to capture all folios across fund houses. Include direct stocks, EPF/PPF and FDs if your plan covers them.
  3. Classify each fund by asset classPure equity and debt funds are simple. Split hybrid funds by their actual mix from the monthly factsheet (a balanced advantage fund at 55% equity counts as 55% equity, 45% debt).
  4. Calculate current percentages and driftDivide each asset class by the total. Compare with your target and see which ones are outside the band.
  5. Work out the amountsTarget value = total × target %. The difference from current value is how much to buy or sell. The calculator below does this for you.
  6. Pick the cheapest routePrefer new money first. If you must sell, choose units held longer than 12 months, check the exit load period, and use the ₹1.25 lakh equity LTCG exemption where possible.
  7. Place the transactionsUse Switch or STP within a fund house, or redeem and buy across fund houses. Mind the cut-off times so both legs get the NAV you expect.
  8. Record and update SIPsNote the date and new mix, and adjust ongoing SIP amounts so new money keeps flowing in the target ratio.

Tax and costs to check before selling

Selling to rebalance is a redemption, so it can trigger capital gains tax and exit load. These are the rates for FY 2026-27, unchanged by Budget 2026.

Fund typeShort-term (and rate)Long-term (and rate)What it means for rebalancing
Equity-oriented (65%+ equity)Held under 12 months: 20%Held 12 months or more: 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 lakh a yearSell older units first; keep yearly gains near the ₹1.25 lakh exemption where possible.
Debt funds (bought on or after 1 April 2023)Taxed at your income slab rate regardless of holding periodHolding period doesn’t change tax; consider your slab and the fund’s exit load.
Gold, international and hybrid (35–65% equity)Under 24 months (12 for listed ETFs): slab rateBeyond that: 12.5%, no ₹1.25 lakh exemptionHolding period matters; check the specific fund’s classification.

Exit load

Many equity funds charge about 1% if redeemed within a year. Liquid funds have a small load for the first 7 days. Check the scheme document.

Stamp duty

0.005% is charged on every purchase, including switch-ins and STP instalments. Small, but it adds up with frequent trades.

Offset with losses

If some funds are at a loss, selling them in the same year can offset gains elsewhere, within the set-off rules.

Time it with the year

The ₹1.25 lakh exemption resets every financial year. Spreading a large rebalance across March and April can use two years’ exemptions.

Common mistakes

Rebalancing too oftenMonthly tinkering mostly adds tax, exit loads and short-term gains. Once a year is enough for most people.
Ignoring tax and exit loadSelling units held for 11 months to fix a 3-point drift can cost more than the drift itself.
Counting hybrid funds as pure equityUse the factsheet split, or your equity share will be overstated.
Chasing last year’s winnerRebalancing means trimming the winner. Moving more money into it does the opposite.
Rebalancing between funds, not assetsSwapping one large-cap fund for another doesn’t change your allocation. Rebalance across asset classes first.
Forgetting the SIPsIf SIPs keep flowing in the old ratio, the drift returns quickly. Update them after each rebalance.

Summary

What

Restoring your target asset mix after markets have pushed it off course.

Why

Keeps risk where you chose it and enforces selling high and buying low.

When

Check once a year (or after big moves) and act only if an asset is beyond your band, often ±5 points.

How

Redirect new money first, then use Switch or STP within an AMC, then redeem and reinvest. SWP from the overweight side if you’re withdrawing.

Watch the cost

Equity STCG 20%, LTCG 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh, debt at slab rate, plus exit loads and stamp duty.

Keep it going

Update SIPs to the target ratio so the portfolio stays balanced between reviews.
