Five ways money moves in a mutual fund

Every mutual fund transaction is either money going in, money coming out, or money moving between schemes, once or on a schedule. Here’s how each one is placed, step by step, and what happens after you hit submit.

Lumpsum One payment from your bank into a scheme. SIP A fixed amount from your bank into a scheme, every period. STP A fixed amount moved from one scheme to another, every period. SWP A fixed amount redeemed from a scheme to your bank, every period. Switch Units moved from one scheme or plan to another, once.

Money in, once

LumpsumOne-time purchase

You invest the whole amount in a single transaction. Units are allotted at the NAV of the day your money reaches the fund (subject to the cut-off time). It suits investors with a windfall, a bonus, or a long horizon who are comfortable with the entry price on one day.

How it’s placed

  1. Complete KYCPAN, Aadhaar-linked KYC with a KRA, a registered bank account, mobile and email. This is a one-time step for all fund houses.
  2. Pick the channelAMC website or app, MF Central, an investment platform or broker, an exchange platform (BSE StAR MF, NSE NMF II) through a distributor, or a physical form at an AMC or RTA (CAMS / KFintech) branch.
  3. Choose scheme, plan and optionSelect the scheme, then Direct or Regular plan, and Growth or IDCW option. Check the minimum amount (often ₹100 to ₹5,000).
  4. Enter amount and folioInvest in an existing folio or let a new one be created. Confirm nominee details.
  5. PayNet banking, UPI, NEFT/RTGS, or cheque/DD for offline forms. Payment must come from your own registered bank account; third-party payments are rejected.
  6. AuthenticateApprove the order with the OTP or link sent to your registered mobile/email.
  7. Units allottedAllotted at the applicable NAV once funds are realised. A confirmation (account statement) arrives by email/SMS, and the holding shows in your CAS.
NAV applied
Same-day NAV if the application and funds both reach the fund before cut-off (3:00 PM for most schemes); otherwise the next business day.
Charges
No entry load. Stamp duty of 0.005% is deducted from the purchase amount.
Mandate needed?
No. You pay each time.
Risk to watch
Timing: the whole amount enters at one price. An STP is a common way to soften this.
Example: You invest ₹1,00,000 in an equity fund at 1:45 PM via UPI and the money is credited before 3 PM. If that day’s NAV is ₹50, you get 2,000 units (minus stamp duty).

Money in, on a schedule

SIPSystematic Investment Plan

A fixed amount is debited from your bank on a chosen date and invested automatically. Because you buy more units when prices are low and fewer when they’re high, SIPs average out your purchase cost over time (rupee cost averaging) and build a saving habit.

How it’s placed

  1. Register a bank mandateSet up an e-NACH, UPI AutoPay or physical NACH mandate with a maximum debit limit. e-NACH and UPI AutoPay are approved online; a paper mandate can take a couple of weeks.
  2. Choose the schemeScheme, plan (Direct/Regular) and option (Growth/IDCW), as for a lumpsum.
  3. Set SIP detailsInstalment amount, frequency (daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly), SIP date, start month and end date or “until cancelled”. Optional: a step-up (top-up) that raises the amount every year.
  4. Pay the first instalment (optional)Many platforms let you pay the first instalment immediately, then the mandate takes over.
  5. Authenticate and registerApprove with OTP. The AMC registers the SIP, which usually takes a few working days before the first auto-debit.
  6. Automatic debitsOn each SIP date, the amount is debited and units are allotted at that day’s NAV, subject to fund realisation and cut-off.
  7. Manage itPause, modify (where allowed), or cancel at any time. Cancellation needs some lead time before the next debit date.
Minimum
Often ₹100 to ₹500 per instalment, with a minimum number of instalments set by the scheme.
NAV applied
NAV of the day the debited money is realised by the fund (before cut-off).
Failed debits
Insufficient balance means no units that month, and your bank may levy a bounce charge. Several consecutive failures can lead the AMC to cancel the SIP.
Tax and exit load
Each instalment is a separate purchase. Holding period, exit load and capital gains are counted per instalment on a first-in, first-out basis.
Example: ₹5,000 every 5th of the month. NAV ₹50 in January gives 100 units; NAV ₹40 in February gives 125 units. Your average cost becomes about ₹44.44, below the simple average NAV of ₹45.

Scheme to scheme, on a schedule

STPSystematic Transfer Plan

You park a lump sum in one scheme (usually a liquid or short-duration debt fund) and transfer a fixed amount from it into another scheme (usually equity) at regular intervals. It combines lumpsum convenience with SIP-style averaging, while the parked money keeps earning debt-fund returns.

How it’s placed

  1. Invest in the source schemePlace a lumpsum in the source fund. STP only works within the same fund house, so both schemes must belong to the same AMC.
  2. Choose source and targetSelect the source (transferor) scheme and the target (transferee) scheme, plan and option.
  3. Set STP detailsTransfer amount, frequency (daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly), date, number of instalments or end date. The source must meet the AMC’s minimum balance.
  4. Pick the STP typeFixed STP (same amount each time), capital appreciation STP (only the gains move), or flex/value STP offered by some AMCs (amount varies with market levels).
  5. AuthenticateApprove with OTP. No bank mandate is needed because no money leaves the fund house.
  6. Automatic transfersOn each date, units are redeemed from the source at its NAV and the proceeds buy units in the target at its applicable NAV.
What happens legally
Each transfer is a redemption from the source plus a purchase in the target.
Tax
Each transfer-out can create capital gains (usually small for liquid funds) that are taxable.
Exit load
Charged on the source if its exit-load period applies. Liquid funds have a graded load only for the first 7 days.
Stops when
Instalments are completed, the source balance runs out, or you cancel.
Example: You receive ₹6,00,000 and put it in a liquid fund, then set an STP of ₹50,000 a month into a flexi-cap fund of the same AMC for 12 months. The unmoved balance keeps earning liquid-fund returns.

Money out, on a schedule

SWPSystematic Withdrawal Plan

The reverse of a SIP. A fixed amount is redeemed from your investment at regular intervals and credited to your bank account. Retirees and anyone needing a steady income use it, while the remaining units stay invested.

How it’s placed

  1. Hold units in the schemeYou need an existing investment large enough to meet the SWP minimum balance and amount.
  2. Choose scheme and folioSelect the scheme and folio from which money will be withdrawn.
  3. Set SWP detailsWithdrawal amount, frequency (monthly or quarterly are common), date, start and end date or number of instalments.
  4. Pick the SWP typeFixed SWP (same amount each time) or appreciation SWP (only the gains are withdrawn, protecting the original capital).
  5. Confirm payout bankProceeds always go to your registered bank account in the folio.
  6. AuthenticateApprove with OTP (two-factor authentication is mandatory for redemptions).
  7. Automatic redemptionsOn each date, units worth the amount are redeemed at that day’s NAV and the money is credited within the SEBI payout timeline (a few working days).
NAV applied
NAV of the SWP date, under the 3:00 PM redemption cut-off.
Tax
Only the gains portion of each withdrawal is taxed, not the whole amount. This often makes SWP more tax-efficient than IDCW payouts.
Exit load
Applies to units redeemed within the exit-load period, so plan the start date accordingly.
Watch out for
Withdrawing more than the fund earns eats into capital, and falling markets mean more units are sold for the same amount.
Example: A ₹20,00,000 balanced advantage fund with an SWP of ₹12,000 a month withdraws about 7.2% a year. If the fund returns more than that over time, the corpus keeps growing.

Scheme to scheme, once

SwitchOne-time transfer between schemes

A switch moves all or part of your holding from one scheme or plan to another within the same fund house, in one go, without the money touching your bank account. Common uses are rebalancing, moving from a Regular to a Direct plan, or changing from IDCW to Growth.

How it’s placed

  1. Select the switch-out schemePick the scheme, plan and folio you’re moving out of.
  2. Select the switch-in schemeChoose the target scheme, plan (Direct/Regular) and option (Growth/IDCW) of the same AMC.
  3. Enter the quantitySwitch by amount, by number of units, or all units. The amount must meet the target scheme’s minimum.
  4. Review the impactCheck the exit load and capital gains that the switch-out will trigger before confirming.
  5. AuthenticateApprove with OTP; the time of verification decides the applicable NAV.
  6. ProcessingUnits are redeemed from the source at its NAV, and the proceeds buy units in the target at its applicable NAV. A confirmation shows both legs.
NAV applied
Switch-out follows the source’s redemption cut-off; switch-in follows the target’s purchase cut-off and is based on when the redemption proceeds become available to it.
Tax
A switch is a sale for tax purposes, even between plans of the same scheme (such as Regular to Direct).
Exit load
Applies on the switch-out if within the load period. Stamp duty applies on the switch-in.
Across fund houses?
Not possible. To move between AMCs, redeem from one and make a fresh purchase in the other.
Example: You hold 1,000 units of a fund’s Regular plan bought three years ago. Switching to its Direct plan lowers the expense ratio going forward, but the gains so far become taxable as long-term capital gains.

Summary

The five transactions differ on just three questions: where the money comes from, where it goes, and whether it happens once or repeats. The table puts them side by side.

Type Money moves Frequency Bank mandate Taxable event Exit load possible Best for
Lumpsum Bank → scheme Once No No (purchase) No Deploying a large sum at once, long horizon
SIP Bank → scheme Recurring Yes (NACH / UPI AutoPay) No (purchase) No Investing from monthly income, rupee cost averaging
STP Scheme → scheme (same AMC) Recurring No Yes, each transfer-out Yes, on source Staggering a lump sum into equity
SWP Scheme → bank Recurring No Yes, each withdrawal (gains only) Yes Regular income, retirement cash flow
Switch Scheme → scheme (same AMC) Once No Yes, on switch-out Yes, on source Rebalancing, Regular to Direct, changing option
Money in

Lumpsum and SIP are pure purchases. No tax at entry, only stamp duty.

Money moving

STP and Switch are a redemption plus a purchase, so tax and exit load apply on the way out.

Money out

SWP is a series of redemptions. Only the gain in each withdrawal is taxed.

Recurring needs setup

SIP needs a bank mandate; STP and SWP need only an instruction to the AMC.

Rules that apply to every transaction

Cut-off times decide the NAV

Before cut-off on a business day gets that day’s NAV; after it gets the next business day’s.

Purchase, most schemes3:00 PM
Purchase, liquid and overnight1:30 PM
Redemption / switch-out3:00 PM

Purchases depend on fund realisation

For any purchase, SIP instalment, STP-in or switch-in, the NAV applies only once the money is actually credited to the scheme’s account, not just when you click pay.

Two-factor authentication

Redemptions, switches, STPs and SWPs need an OTP sent to your registered mobile and email. The NAV depends on when it’s verified.

KYC and folio details

Valid KYC, PAN, a registered bank account and nominee (or opt-out) are required. Payouts only go to the registered bank account.

Where to track

Each transaction generates an account statement from the AMC/RTA. A monthly Consolidated Account Statement (CAS) from CAMS, KFintech or the depositories covers all funds in one place.

Read the scheme documents

Minimum amounts, exit loads and STP/SWP options differ by scheme. The Scheme Information Document (SID) and KIM have the exact numbers.
